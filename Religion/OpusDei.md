# St José Marie Esquivar

[St. Josemaría Escrivá de Balaguer](https://www.britannica.com/biography/St-Josemaria-Escriva-de-Balaguer)


St. Josemaría Escrivá (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0pkw_&q=St+Jos%C3%A9+Marie+Esquivar+st.+josemar%C3%ADa+escriv%C3%A1) (often misspelled as St. José Marie Esquivar) was a Spanish Roman Catholic priest (1902–1975) who founded Opus Dei. Canonized by Pope John Paul II in 2002, he is widely known as the "saint of ordinary life" for his teachings on finding holiness through everyday work and family duties. [1] (https://opusdei.org/en-nz/saint-josemaria/), [2] (https://www.facebook.com/DioceseofLafayette/posts/saint-of-the-day-saint-josemar%C3%ADa-escriv%C3%A1-de-balaguer-19021975-was-a-spanish-cath/1552119623615870/)

Key Facts & Timeline

• Birth & Ordination. Born José María Mariano Escrivá y Albás on January 9, 1902, in Barbastro, Spain, he was ordained a priest in Zaragoza on March 28, 1925. [1] (https://stjosemaria.org/biography/), [2] (https://opusdei.org/en/article/saint-josemaria-biography-family-childhood/)
• Founding of Opus Dei. On October 2, 1928, in Madrid, he founded Opus Dei ("Work of God"), an organization dedicated to teaching that laypeople can achieve saintliness in their daily secular lives. [1] (https://www.britannica.com/biography/St-Josemaria-Escriva-de-Balaguer), [2] (https://escriva.org/en/page/saint-josemaria/), [3] (https://angelusnews.com/faith/josemaria-escriva)
• Move to Rome. After surviving the Spanish Civil War, he relocated to Rome in 1946 to oversee the international expansion of Opus Dei across five continents. [1] (https://www.isje.org/en/life-of-st-josemaria-escriva-1902-1975/)
• Death & Canonization. He died in Rome on June 26, 1975, and was canonized on October 6, 2002, in St. Peter's Square. His liturgical feast day is celebrated on June 26. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1), [2] (https://www.ap.church/saints/st--josemar%C3%ADa-escriv%C3%A1)
• Patronage. In addition to Opus Dei, he is recognized as a patron saint of people with diabetes, a condition from which he suffered and was reportedly cured. [1] (https://stjosemaria.org/product/medal-and-prayer-card-of-st-josemaria-escriva-patron-of-diabetics/), [2] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1)

Core Teachings & Notable Works

• Sanctification of Work. Escrivá emphasized the "universal call to holiness," teaching that ordinary, honest labor and mundane daily tasks can be transformed into prayer and offerings to God.
• Famous Writings. His best-known book is The Way (Camino), a collection of 999 spiritual maxims first published in 1939 that has sold millions of copies worldwide. Other notable works include Furrow, The Forge, and Christ Is Passing By.
