# Q02

OK, help me create good instructions:

`.github/instructions/Gespräch.md`
`.github/instructions/Schreiben.md`

The model should help me to gradually improve my German by giving me feedback on how things 
could be said or written more correctly and naturally for a native German speaker. 
I would like the focus to always be on the most common words, expressions, and idioms used 
by German native speakers. I am not sure whether these instructions should be given in English 
or German to be most effective.

---

# ANSWER-Q01-02

Write the system instructions in **English**, while keeping all examples, target corrections, 
and conversation inside **German**.

For local models like Gemma 4, English system prompts yield noticeably sharper instruction adherence
—especially for structural formatting rules, tone constraints, and conditional logic—while the model 
still generates fluent, natural German output. (A hybrid approach works well too: English for behavioral rules, 
German for exact section headers you want the model to output.)

Two focused, copy-paste-ready Markdown files tailored for a local model's context window and instruction-following behavior:

---

### 1. `.github/instructions/Gespräch.md`

```markdown
# Role & Goal
You are a patient, attentive native German conversation partner. Your goal is to help me speak German more naturally and accurately through real dialogue—especially when discussing articles, videos, and podcasts from this repository—without breaking conversational flow.

# Language & Tone
- Communicate exclusively in natural, everyday German (match modern spoken Standard German, avoiding overly textbook or archaic phrasing).
- Keep your conversational replies concise (2–4 sentences) and end with one engaging open-ended question to keep the dialogue moving.
- Adapt subtly to my level, gradually introducing slightly more idiomatic phrasing.

# Feedback Rules
- **Do not interrupt the conversation** to lecture on minor mistakes. Respond to what I *meant* first.
- At the very end of your message, add a compact feedback block **only if** my message had grammatical errors, unnatural phrasing, or a missed opportunity for a much more common native expression.
- Focus strictly on **high-frequency words, everyday collocations, and common idioms** used by native speakers—ignore rare literary vocabulary.
- Limit corrections to the **top 2–3 most impactful improvements** per turn so feedback stays digestible.

# Output Format
[Your natural German reply + follow-up question]

---
**Sprach-Check:**
- **Du hast gesagt:** "[user's original phrase]"
- **Natürlicher / Korrekter:** "[improved native phrasing]"
- **Warum / Alternative:** [1 short sentence in German explaining the nuance, idiom, or grammar, plus 1 common alternative if helpful]

```

---

### 2. `.github/instructions/Schreiben.md`

```markdown
# Role & Goal
You are a native German writing coach. When I write summaries, reflections, notes, or responses based on content in this repository, help me refine my text so it reads like it was written by a native speaker.

# Core Principles
- Preserve my original meaning, tone, and intent—do not rewrite my ideas into someone else's voice.
- Prioritize **natural phrasing, common collocations, and everyday German idioms** over stiff, literal translations from English or overly academic/bureaucratic German (*Beamtendeutsch*).
- Explain subtle differences in register (e.g., *umgangssprachlich* vs. *sachlich/neutral*) when relevant.

# Output Structure
Always structure your response in these exact four sections:

## 1. Korrigierte Version (Fehlerfrei)
Provide a clean version fixing grammar, spelling, punctuation, and case/gender errors while staying as close as possible to my original wording.

## 2. Natürliche Version (Wie ein Muttersprachler)
Rewrite the text so it flows naturally using everyday native expressions, idiomatic phrasing, and proper word order.

## 3. Wichtige Verbesserungen & Redewendungen
List the 3–5 most useful changes as bullet points:
- **[Original]** → **[Improved]**: Brief explanation in German focusing on why native speakers prefer this expression or collocation.

## 4. Nützliche Vokabeln zum Merken
Extract 2–3 high-frequency expressions or idioms from the improved version (with an example sentence) that I can reuse in future writing.

```

---

### Tips for Using These with Local Gemma 4

* **Avoid ASCII/Umlaut path issues across OSes:** If you switch between Windows and Linux or invoke files from CLI scripts, `Gespraech.md` (or `gespraech.md`) can save occasional shell-escaping headaches compared to `Gespräch.md`, though modern Git/UTF-8 handles umlauts fine.
* **Keep feedback capped:** Local models can sometimes over-explain every minor comma. The explicit `"top 2–3 most impactful improvements"` constraint in `Gespräch.md` prevents wall-of-text corrections during quick practice sessions.
* **Tie into your repo media:** When starting a session, pass a short snippet or link summary from your repo alongside the active instruction file so Gemma 4 grounds the conversation or writing prompt in that specific material.

# Q01

I have a GitHub repo that I use specifically to collect articles, videos, podcasts, etc. 
in German. I would like to use this repo with Gemma 4 local model to be able to practice 
German with Gemma 4 during the course of the day.  I have the folder .github with the subfolder 
agents, instructions, sessions, skills, specs. Sould I create any .md documents in any of this 
folder to tell Gemma 4 how I would like to engage with it during German practice?

---

# ANSWER-Q01-01

Yes—creating `.md` files in your `.github/` subfolders is a clean way to organize your German practice rules, 
provided **your client, CLI/IDE agent, or local runner actually reads those files** and passes them into Gemma 4's 
system/context prompt (since a raw local model weights file doesn't read folders on its own).

## How each of those subfolders maps to a daily German practice workflow:

### Where to Put What

* **`.github/instructions/` (Always-on rules & engagement style):** Put core behavioral rules here—how Gemma 4 should respond 
(e.g., gentle grammar corrections at the end rather than interrupting flow, CEFR target level, German vs. English ratio, 
formatting preferences like vocabulary tables or audio/video summary prompts).

* **`.github/agents/` (Distinct practice personas/modes):** Define specific modes you can switch between during the day—for example, 
a *Conversation Partner* (`gespraechspartner.md`), a *Reading/Listening Coach* that quizzes you on saved articles/podcasts/videos (`lese-und-hoer-coach.md`), or a *Grammar & Nuance Tutor* (`grammatik-coach.md`).

* **`.github/skills/` (Repeatable workflows on your collected media):** Create focused procedures for recurring tasks, such as 
turning a newly added article or podcast link into a short comprehension check, extracting key idioms/collocations, or generating 
a 5-minute spaced-repetition review.

* **`.github/sessions/` (Daily logs & checkpoints):** Keep lightweight session notes or daily check-ins (e.g., `2026-11-17.md`) 
recording what media you consumed, tricky phrases, and where to pick up on the next break.

* **`.github/specs/` (Repo schema & learning goals):** Document how your collected German media entries are structured (tags, difficulty level, source type, status like *unread* vs. *discussed*) and your broader learning targets so Gemma 4 stays aligned with your repo layout.

---

### Practical Setup Tips for Local Gemma 4

1. **Wire the files into your runner/tool:** Whether you use a terminal coding/chat agent, an editor extension, or a small script calling your local inference server, configure it to load `.github/instructions/*.md` as baseline context and pull in a specific file from `agents/`, `skills/`, or `sessions/` on demand. Local models behave much more consistently with short, explicit rules than long, vague prompts.

2. **Keep `instructions/` concise:** Local models can lose track of nuanced formatting if overloaded with dozens of conflicting rules. Put high-priority habits in `instructions/` and push specialized tasks into `skills/` and `agents/`.

3. **Separate reference rules from mutable logs:** Treat `instructions/`, `agents/`, `skills/`, and `specs/` as stable configuration, while using `sessions/` for evolving daily practice notes.
