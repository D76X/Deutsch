# "When is the best period to harvest chestnuts in 2026 South Tyrol?"

The best time to harvest chestnuts in South Tyrol in 2026 is from `early October to early November`, 
when the fruits reach peak ripeness and drop naturally from the trees.

Generally, the overall season spans from late September until mid-November, aligning with the 
famous autumn tradition known as `Törggelen`. For this year, the region's main chestnut-themed 
events are scheduled during this exact timeframe: the `Eisacktal Chestnut Weeks` will take place 
`from October 17 to November 1, 2026`, while the `Keschtnriggl` chestnut days run `from October 15 to November 8, 2026`.

## Ideal Locations and Hiking Trails for Harvesting

The areas richest in chestnut groves are concentrated along the `Eisack Valley (Valle Isarco)`, the Adige Valley (Valle dell'Adige), 
and around `Merano`. The primary point of reference is the `Keschtnweg (the Chestnut Trail)`, a route of about `90 kilometres running` 
from the `Neustift Abbey (near Brixen)` down to `Terlan and Vilpian`, crossing the Ritten plateau.

The most popular sections to combine a hike with chestnut picking include:

* The trail from Feldthurns (Velturno) to Klausen (Chiusa): an easy walk of about 1 hour and 40 minutes, which is highly popular with families.
* The surroundings of Villanders (Villandro) and Barbian (Barbiano): areas densely packed with traditional farms and broadleaf forests.

## Rules and Useful Advice for Harvesting

To gather chestnuts safely and in compliance with local regulations, keep these fundamental guidelines in mind:

* Gather only fallen fruit: Ripe chestnuts are easy to spot because their husks split open and they drop to the ground on their own. Never shake the branches or pick unripe fruit directly from the tree.
* Respect private property: In South Tyrol, many historic chestnut groves belong to local heritage farms (Masi), and harvesting might be reserved for the owners. It is essential to only collect fruit in public forests or along marked public trails where foraging is permitted, while respecting daily weight limits (usually up to 2 kg per person).
* Equipment: Wearing sturdy hiking shoes is recommended for steep terrain, and bringing thick gloves will help protect your hands from the prickly husks.
