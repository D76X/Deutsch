# The Miracle of Our Lady of Guadalupe

[Our Lady of Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe)   

The Miracle of Our Lady of Guadalupe refers to the December 1531 apparitions of the Virgin Mary to an indigenous convert named Juan Diego on Tepeyac Hill near Mexico City. When Juan Diego opened his cloak (tilma) before the bishop, fresh winter roses fell out, and an unexplained, enduring image of the Virgin miraculously appeared on the fabric. [1] (https://www.denvercatholic.org/our-lady-of-guadalupe-the-miracle-that-changed-history), [2] (https://thewaywardcatholic.com/2025/12/27/the-miracles-of-our-lady-of-guadalupe-explained/), [3] (https://www.britannica.com/question/What-was-the-miracle-of-Our-Lady-of-Guadalupe)

The Apparitions

• Dates: December 9 through December 12, 1531.
• Location: Tepeyac Hill, on the edge of modern Mexico City.
• Visionary: St. Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin, an indigenous Nahua convert.
• Request: The Virgin Mary asked for a sacred shrine to be built on the hill to show her love and compassion. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe), [2] (https://www.nationalshrine.org/blog/st-juan-diego-and-the-miracle-of-our-lady-of-guadalupe/), [3] (https://guadalupeshrine.org/our-lady-of-guadalupe-shrine/), [4] (https://www.catholicjourneys.com/unveil-the-miracles-of-our-lady-on-a-guadalupe-pilgrimage-in-mexico/), [5] (https://www.britannica.com/question/What-was-the-miracle-of-Our-Lady-of-Guadalupe)

The Miraculous Sign

• The Roses: To convince the skeptical bishop (Juan de Zumárraga), Mary made out-of-season, native Castilian roses bloom on the freezing winter hillside. [1] (https://www.catholicjourneys.com/unveil-the-miracles-of-our-lady-on-a-guadalupe-pilgrimage-in-mexico/), [2] (https://www.denvercatholic.org/our-lady-of-guadalupe-the-miracle-that-changed-history)
• The Tilma Image: When Juan Diego poured the roses from his cactus-fiber cloak, a life-sized, olive-skinned image of the Virgin was found imprinted on the material. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe), [2] (https://thewaywardcatholic.com/2025/12/27/the-miracles-of-our-lady-of-guadalupe-explained/)
• Preservation: The coarse fabric has defied normal decay, surviving for nearly 500 years in humid conditions without losing its vibrant colors. [1] (https://www.britannica.com/question/What-was-the-miracle-of-Our-Lady-of-Guadalupe), [2] (https://thewaywardcatholic.com/2025/12/27/the-miracles-of-our-lady-of-guadalupe-explained/)

Historical & Cultural Impact

• Mass Conversions: The event helped bridge indigenous and Spanish cultures, leading millions of Aztec people to embrace Catholicism. [1] (https://ewtn.co.uk/chpop-7-miracles-that-reveal-our-lady-of-guadalupes-powerful-intercession/), [2] (https://app.formed.org/app/videos/578780)
• The Basilica: The original tilma is preserved today inside the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe in Mexico City, one of the most-visited Catholic shrines in the world. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe)
• Feast Day: Celebrated annually on December 12. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe)

## Sant Angelo Texas the rosary given to the indians

In San Angelo, Texas, the "Lady in Blue"—Venerable Sor María de Ágreda, a Spanish Franciscan nun—reportedly appeared to the Jumano Native Americans in the 1620s through mystical bilocation. She instructed them in the Catholic faith and taught them to pray, leading them to request baptism from Franciscan priests. [1] (https://www.catholic.org/news/hf/faith/story.php?id=76029), [2] (https://texashighways.com/travel-news/in-san-angelo-a-celebration-remembers-the-mysterious-lady-in-blue-who-evangelized-to-jumanos/), [3] (https://aleteia.org/2017/08/15/the-bi-locating-nun-who-may-have-evangelized-america/), [4] (https://www.marfapublicradio.org/show/nature-notes/2019-08-15/the-lady-in-blue-a-mystical-encounter-in-west-texas-echoes-across-centuries-continents)

The Legend of the Lady in Blue

• The Vision: In the 1620s, Sor María de Ágreda (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F070gvk&q=Sor+Mar%C3%ADa+de+%C3%81greda) claimed she was transported in spirit (bilocated) over 500 times from her cloistered convent in Ágreda, Spain, to the American Southwest. [1] (https://aleteia.org/2017/08/15/the-bi-locating-nun-who-may-have-evangelized-america/), [2] (https://www.catholic.org/news/hf/faith/story.php?id=76029)
• The Jumano Delegation: In 1629, a delegation of Jumano Indians arrived at a mission in Isleta, New Mexico, carrying handmade crosses and asking for priests to visit their homeland near the Concho River in modern-day San Angelo, Texas. [1] (https://authentictexas.com/the-mysterious-lady-in-blue/), [2] (https://texashighways.com/travel-news/in-san-angelo-a-celebration-remembers-the-mysterious-lady-in-blue-who-evangelized-to-jumanos/), [3] (https://www.marfapublicradio.org/show/nature-notes/2019-08-15/the-lady-in-blue-a-mystical-encounter-in-west-texas-echoes-across-centuries-continents)
• The Encounter: The Jumano told Spanish missionaries they had never seen European men before, but were taught the faith and given prayers by a young woman wearing a brilliant blue habit and cape. [1] (https://authentictexas.com/the-mysterious-lady-in-blue/), [2] (https://www.catholic.org/news/hf/faith/story.php?id=76029)
• The Baptism: Franciscan friars traveled with the Jumanos to the San Angelo area and baptized roughly 2,000 Indigenous people who were already prepared in the faith. [1] (https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2017/09/14/did-this-spanish-nun-bi-locate-to-texas-vatican-aims-to-find-out/), [2] (https://authentictexas.com/the-mysterious-lady-in-blue/), [3] (https://www.marfapublicradio.org/show/nature-notes/2019-08-15/the-lady-in-blue-a-mystical-encounter-in-west-texas-echoes-across-centuries-continents)

Investigation and Legacy

• Sor María’s Account: When Franciscan investigator Alonso de Benavides traveled to Spain to check the story, he met Sor María. She accurately described the geography, plants, and people of West Texas and named the Jumano tribe. [1] (https://sanangelodiocese.org/lady-in-blue), [2] (https://www.marfapublicradio.org/show/nature-notes/2019-08-15/the-lady-in-blue-a-mystical-encounter-in-west-texas-echoes-across-centuries-continents)
• Vatican Interest: The Catholic Church has studied her cause for canonization, and local historians and the Diocese of San Angelo continue to commemorate the historic encounter. [1] (https://epicpew.com/vatican-cecchin-apparitions-texas/), [2] (https://texashighways.com/travel-news/in-san-angelo-a-celebration-remembers-the-mysterious-lady-in-blue-who-evangelized-to-jumanos/), [3] (https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2017/09/14/did-this-spanish-nun-bi-locate-to-texas-vatican-aims-to-find-out/), [4] (https://sanangelodiocese.org/lady-in-blue)

# St. Louis de Montfort

St. Louis de Montfort (Louis-Marie Grignion de Montfort, 1673–1716) was a French Catholic priest and missionary. He is best known for promoting devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary, the Rosary, and total consecration to Jesus through Mary, and was canonized in 1947. 

[1] (https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-Louis-Marie-Grignion-de-Montfort), 
[2] (https://www.facebook.com/DioceseofLafayette/posts/saint-of-the-day-saint-louis-mary-de-montfort-16731716-was-a-french-catholic-pri/1495693862591780/), 
[3] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MapUfDJKxjo)

Life and Ministry

• Birth and Early Life: Born on January 31, 1673, in Montfort-sur-Meu, France. [1] (https://www.franciscanmedia.org/saint-of-the-day/saint-louis-mary-grignion-de-montfort/)
• Education: Studied at the Jesuit College of St. Thomas Becket in Rennes and the Seminary of Saint-Sulpice in Paris. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Montfort), [2] (https://www.montfort.org.uk/life_summary.php)
• Priesthood: Ordained a diocesan priest on June 5, 1700, and later named a Missionary Apostolic by Pope Clement XI. [1] (https://mycatholic.life/saints/saints-of-the-liturgical-year/april-28-saint-louis-grignon-de-montfort-priest/), [2] (https://www.montfort.org.uk/life_summary.php)
• Preaching: Traveled on foot across western France to preach parish missions, revive faith among the poor, and combat Jansenism. [1] (https://ewtn.co.uk/article-saint-louis-marie-grignion-de-montfort-2/), [2] (https://www.montfort.org.uk/life_summary.php), [3] (https://www.franciscanmedia.org/saint-of-the-day/saint-louis-mary-grignion-de-montfort/)
• Death: Died on April 28, 1716, at Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre, where his tomb rests in the local basilica. [1] (https://www.franciscanmedia.org/saint-of-the-day/saint-louis-mary-grignion-de-montfort/)

Major Works

• True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary: His seminal spiritual work outlining the path of Marian consecration. [1] (https://ewtn.co.uk/article-saint-louis-marie-grignion-de-montfort-2/)
• The Secret of Mary: A shorter text detailing the surrender of one's life to Jesus through Mary.
• The Secret of the Rosary: A guide on how to pray the Rosary with depth and focus.

Religious Foundations & Legacy

• Company of Mary: Also known as the Montfort Missionaries, founded to continue parish missions.
• Daughters of Wisdom: A religious congregation co-founded with Blessed Marie-Louise of Jesus to care for the sick and educate the poor.
• Feast Day: Celebrated annually on April 28.
• Papal Influence: His writings heavily inspired Pope John Paul II, whose papal motto Totus Tuus ("Totally Yours") was drawn from Montfort's spirituality. [1] (https://sldm.org/our-saint/), [2] (https://www.montfortian.info/ttjnew/brief-profile-of-st.-louis-marie-de-montfort.html), [3] (https://ewtn.co.uk/article-saint-louis-marie-grignion-de-montfort-2/), [4] (https://www.franciscanmedia.org/saint-of-the-day/saint-louis-mary-grignion-de-montfort/)
